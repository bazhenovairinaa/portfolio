01 / Concept & Form

Natural vs. Industrial Interconnection

"Kankei" (关系/関係 — relationship / connection) is a table lamp exploring the dialogue between raw natural timber and industrial elements. Translating fundamental spatial tension into a tactile lighting object, it investigates how unrefined materials co-exist with engineered light.

02 / Art Philosophy

Mono-ha Aesthetics & Spatial Dialogue

Rooted in the 1960s–70s Japanese-Korean Mono-ha ("School of Things") movement, the object focuses on three core pillars: (1) The meeting of contrasting materials, (2) Emptiness as an active spatial element, and (3) The transience and fragility of physical form.

Mono-ha Philosophy & Material Encounters

Mono-ha artists rejected traditional decorative artwork, choosing instead to present natural wood, stone, glass, and metal in raw state. In Kankei, the entire manufacturing process — from initial timber sawing to final finishing — was executed by hand, symbolizing an intimate dialogue between creator, material, and surrounding void.

Material Selection & Siberian Larch Grain

High-density Siberian larch was selected for its durability and distinct growth ring patterns. The wood is stained to reveal the graphic depth of the grain while preserving its natural origin and organic warmth.

Hand-Chiseling & Tactile Craftsmanship

The primary circular apertures in the solid wood block were drilled using a hole saw attachment, then painstakingly carved and refined by hand using wood chisels. Intentionally unpolished, natural edge imperfections emphasize transience, transforming a functional lamp into a unique sculptural installation.

Concealed Light Routing & Downward Diffusion

The warm LED ribbon is embedded within custom-carved internal grooves. The structure is engineered so the primary light flux is directed downward onto the larch mass, casting warm ambient reflections that accentuate the wood's tactile relief.
